P-N Junction — put P-type and N-type silicon together. A depletion region forms at the boundary. Forward bias shrinks it and lets current flow; reverse bias widens it and blocks current. This is a diode.

Key: The depletion region is a barrier of ionised atoms with no free carriers. Forward bias pushes carriers toward the junction, shrinking this barrier. Reverse bias widens it — until it breaks down at very high voltages.
BJT (NPN) Switch — the base current controls a much larger collector current. β (hFE) = I_C / I_B. Small base input → large collector output. This is a current-controlled switch.

Current amplification: A base current of just 50µA controls a collector current of 5mA — a factor of 100×. In switching, we use this to fully cut off or fully saturate the transistor.
N-Channel MOSFET Switch — gate voltage controls an inversion channel. Zero gate current needed. The dominant transistor in all modern digital chips. Billions on one chip possible because gate draws no static current.

Why MOSFET dominates: Zero gate current means billions can switch simultaneously without current overload. The gate only charges a tiny capacitor — and only while switching, not while static.
