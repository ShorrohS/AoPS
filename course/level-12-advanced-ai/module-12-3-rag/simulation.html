RAG vs Vanilla LLM
RAG = Retrieval-Augmented Generation. Instead of relying solely on training-time knowledge, the LLM retrieves relevant documents at query time and uses them as context. This fixes hallucinations for knowledge-intensive tasks and keeps answers grounded in real sources.

Documents must be split into chunks before embedding. Chunk size is a critical hyperparameter. Too small: loss of context. Too large: irrelevant content dilutes the signal. Chunks overlap to preserve context across boundaries.

RAG vs Vanilla LLM — when does retrieval help? A vanilla LLM can hallucinate facts not in training data, produce outdated information, and can't cite sources. RAG grounds the answer in retrieved documents, enables source citation, and can be updated without retraining.
