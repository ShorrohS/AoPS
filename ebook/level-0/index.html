Volume 0
Before Computers

The origins of computation — Level 0 of 12

This volume traces the journey from counting beans to punched holes — from the moment humans first realised arithmetic could break down at scale, to the moment they built a machine that could process fifty million records in six weeks instead of eight years.

Five chapters. No mathematics beyond arithmetic. No electronics. Just the slow, brilliant accumulation of mechanical ideas that made the electronic computer possible — and inevitable.

Herman Hollerith tabulated the 1890 US Census in six weeks instead of eight years — by encoding 62 million people as holes in cards and reading those holes electrically. The company he founded became IBM.

What comes next

Level 1: Physical Computing begins where this volume ends. Gear gave way to the electron. A relay, a vacuum tube, and a transistor are three different answers to the same question: how do you build an electrical switch that is faster, smaller, and cheaper than the one before it? Each generation achieved roughly 1,000× improvement in speed and 1,000× reduction in size. You will follow that exponential trajectory from the 1837 telegraph relay to the 2024 five-nanometre transistor — step by step, with a simulation at every stage.
