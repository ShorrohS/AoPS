Type A — Chapter Hero FIG 0.2-A The Mechanical Calculator — Arithmetic Encoded in Metal and Motion
The two great mechanical calculation inventions side by side. Left: the Pascaline (1642) — six interlocked decimal wheels. Wheel 3 is at digit 9, highlighted in red, about to trigger a carry cascade to wheel 4 (gold). Right: Napier's Bones (1617) — multiplication encoded as rod engravings. Row 6 is selected; diagonal addition of the highlighted cells yields 357 × 6 = 2142.
Type C — Concept Diagram FIG 0.2-B The Mechanical Carry — How 9 + 1 Advances the Next Wheel
The Pascaline carry mechanism in three phases. Step 1: units wheel is at 1, tens wheel is at 9. Step 2: adding 1 causes the units wheel to advance to 2 and overflow, sending a carry pulse to the tens wheel (red). Step 3: the tens wheel has advanced from 9 to 0 (gold), the carry is resolved. This same logical structure — overflow produces a carry to the next column — appears in every binary adder ever built.
Type D — Process Diagram FIG 0.2-C Napier's Bones — How 357 × 6 Works Step by Step
Napier's Bones: 357 × 6 in three steps. Place rods 3, 5, 7. Read row 6: cells show 1/8, 3/0, 4/2. Add diagonals left-to-right: 1 | (8+3) | (0+4) | 2 → carry the 1 from 11 → result 2142. The multiplication problem has been reduced to a sequence of additions — a lookup strategy still used in CPU microcode tables.
Type B — Historical Comparison FIG 0.2-D Mechanical Calculator Lineage — Key Innovations Across Five Machines
The mechanical calculator lineage. Each machine contributed exactly one critical innovation that appears in modern computers: external state storage (registers), lookup encoding (ROM), automatic carry (ALU carry flag), variable gear engagement (barrel shifter), and programmability (stored-program architecture).
Type F — Simulation Figure FIG 0.2-E Virtual Pascaline — Accumulator State: 999 + 1 (Multi-Digit Carry)
The Pascaline's most revealing edge case: adding 1 to 999. All three wheels are at 9 (highlighted red) before. Each carry must wait for the previous wheel to complete its rotation before advancing the next. After: all three wheels have settled to 0 and the thousands wheel has advanced to 1 (gold). This sequential carry delay — O(n) for n carry wheels — motivated the carry lookahead adder designs of the 1950s, still present in every modern CPU.
