Type A — Chapter Hero FIG 0.5-A The Hollerith Tabulating Machine — 1890 US Census
The Hollerith system in operation. Left: the electric tabulating machine — a cabinet of circular counting dials (each incrementing as holes pass under the pin press) with a card press reader at the bottom and sorting compartments below. Right: an annotated 1890 census punch card with field zones and digit rows labelled. Each card encodes one person's demographic record across 80 columns. Bottom: time comparison — 8 years manually versus 6 weeks with the Hollerith system, a 78× improvement that determined the viability of the decennial census.
Type C — Concept Diagram FIG 0.5-B Hollerith Card Anatomy — How 80 Columns Encode Alphanumeric Data
The Hollerith card encoding scheme. Three zone rows (12, 11, 0) at the top and nine digit rows (1–9) below. A letter is encoded by punching a zone row plus a digit row in the same column. A digit is encoded by punching its digit row alone. Column A (zone 12 + digit 1) and column B (zone 12 + digit 2) are shown labelled. This two-punch scheme for letters vs one-punch for digits is a compressed encoding that maximises the information density of 80 physical columns.
Type D — Process Diagram FIG 0.5-C Census Tabulation Workflow — Card to Sorted Count
The complete 1890 census tabulation pipeline. Step 1: field enumerators collect household data on paper forms. Step 2: clerks key-punch the data onto cards. Step 3: cards pass through the Hollerith press — metal pins drop through holes to contact a mercury cup, closing an electrical circuit. Step 4: each closed circuit pulses an electromagnetic counter dial. Step 5: dial readings are transcribed into tabulated totals. The 62 million person census was fully processed in 6 weeks — complete results published 5 years ahead of the 1900 deadline.
Type E — Before vs After FIG 0.5-D Census Efficiency: 1880 Manual vs 1890 Hollerith
The census efficiency comparison quantifies the Hollerith impact precisely. The 1880 census took 8 years with 450 clerks handling 50 million records — an estimated 1.5 million tallying errors accumulated. The 1890 census processed 62 million records (24% more people) in 6 weeks using 56 machines at half the cost. Mechanical counting eliminated the arithmetic error pathway entirely; errors could only enter at the key-punch stage. Hollerith's company later became IBM.
Type F — Simulation Figure FIG 0.5-E Hollerith Card Encoder/Decoder — Text to Card to Decode
Static view of the Module 0.5 encoder/decoder simulation. Input "HELLO" — each letter resolves to a Hollerith zone+digit pair: H=12+8, E=12+5, L=12+3, L=12+3, O=12+6. The generated card shows two holes per column — one in the zone-12 row and one in the digit row. The decoder reads the same card and reconstructs the original five characters. The challenge asks students to encode their own name and decode a mystery card.
