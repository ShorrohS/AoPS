Type A — Chapter Hero FIG 1.6-A Moore's Law — Transistor Count Doubles Every Two Years, 1971–2024
Moore's Law chart on a logarithmic scale, 1971–2023. The dashed line shows the ideal doubling-every-2-years trajectory. Real chip counts follow it with remarkable fidelity across 7 orders of magnitude — from 2,300 transistors (Intel 4004, 1971) to 80 billion (NVIDIA H100, 2023). Three eras are marked: micron-scale microprocessors (1970s–1990s), the submicron transition (2000s), and the sub-10nm FinFET/GAA era (2010s–present). The logarithmic scale makes linear growth on this chart correspond to exponential growth in reality.
Type C — Concept Diagram FIG 1.6-B Physical Limits — When Does Transistor Scaling End?
The physical limits of transistor scaling. The top bar shows relative size from 5nm transistors to a human hair at 70,000nm — the scale compression is extraordinary. Three fundamental limits are approaching: quantum tunnelling (electrons pass through the gate even when off, causing leakage and errors), heat density (more transistors per mm² generates more heat, currently around 100W/cm² for high-performance chips), and the silicon atomic floor (a silicon atom is 0.28nm wide — you cannot build a transistor smaller than the atoms it's made of). The industry response is architectural: 3D chip stacking, specialised accelerators, and new materials like gallium nitride.
Type E — Comparison / Consequence FIG 1.6-C Consequences of Moore's Law — Why 53 Years of Doubling Changed Everything
The consequence of 53 years of exponential transistor growth. Compared against linear improvements in other technologies (cars, aircraft), Moore's Law doubling stands apart. Cost per transistor fell from roughly $300 in 1971 to fractions of a nanodollar today — a 100-billion-fold reduction. This is why a modern smartphone carries more computational power than the entire 1969 Apollo mission, and why AI systems that required rooms full of hardware in 2015 fit on a consumer GPU in 2024. Exponential compounding, sustained long enough, produces qualitatively different civilisations.
